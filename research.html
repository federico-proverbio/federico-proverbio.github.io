Comparing Homeownership in Germany and Italy: Analyzing the Role of Relevant Policies through the Lens of a Quantitative Model

Master's Thesis · 2026

I build on the overlapping-generations model of Kaas (2021) to study whether differences in housing policy can explain differences in homeownership between Germany and Italy along the income distribution and the life cycle. Roughly 40% of the homeownership gap can be explained by lower real-estate transfer taxes and differences in the structure of the two countries' social housing programs. While there is little difference among middle-aged workers, gaps persist among young, middle-wealth workers and retirees.

The Long Shadow of Servitude: The Causal Effect of 19th-Century Slavery on Contemporary Labor Market Inequalities in Brazil

with Federico Eolo Otto Stoppa, Eirini Liontou & Nishant Jha · 2026

We adopt an instrumental-variables design to test whether the historical presence of slavery in Brazil shapes the modern quality and stability of labor-market insertion across ethnicities. We rely on municipal-level micro-data, both historical and modern, and use the AMC harmonization technique to ensure spatially consistent comparisons. We find significant effects on the wage and unemployment gap between white and Black populations, while labor force participation and informality rates show no sizeable differences.
